# Vocal Editing and Mixing Case Study

## Overview

This case study demonstrates my process for taking a raw vocal recording and turning it into a more polished vocal mix.

The before and after files contain the same vocal passage:

- `vocal-mix-before.wav` — raw or minimally processed vocal
- `vocal-mix-after.wav` — vocal processed through the full FL Studio mixer chain

The goal was to improve tonal balance, pitch consistency, dynamic control, clarity, saturation, depth, and overall placement in the mix.

## Software

- DAW: FL Studio
- Main processing chain:
  - Fruity Parametric EQ 2
  - Fruity Parametric EQ 2
  - soothe2
  - Auto-Tune Pro
  - Comp FET-76
  - Fruity Limiter
  - Fruity WaveShaper
  - ValhallaVintageVerb
  - Fruity Parametric EQ 2
  - Fruity Delay 2

## Processing Chain

The vocal was processed in the following order.

### 1. Fruity Parametric EQ 2 — Initial Cleanup

The first EQ stage was used for basic corrective processing before the vocal reached the rest of the chain.

This stage includes:

- Removing unnecessary low-frequencies
- Reducing rumble or proximity buildup
- Cleaning up broad tonal imbalances
- Preparing the vocal for later compression and pitch processing

### 2. Fruity Parametric EQ 2 — Additional Tonal Shaping

A second EQ instance was used for more focused tonal adjustment.

This allowed the cleanup and tonal-shaping decisions to remain separate.

- Reduce low-mid muddiness
- Control nasal or boxy frequencies
- Improve vocal presence
- Create more separation between the vocal and instrumental

Using multiple EQ stages made it easier to apply smaller, more controlled adjustments instead of forcing all tonal changes through one processor.

### 3. soothe2 — Dynamic Resonance Control

soothe2 was used to reduce harsh or resonant frequencies dynamically.

Unlike a static EQ cut, soothe2 reacts only when problematic frequencies become excessive. This helped control:

- Harsh upper-mid frequencies
- Sharp resonances
- Tonal buildup on louder words
- Fatiguing frequencies that changed throughout the performance

The goal was to smooth the vocal without removing too much brightness or clarity.

### 4. Auto-Tune Pro — Pitch Correction

Auto-Tune Pro was used to improve pitch consistency.

The processing was intended to keep the vocal aligned with the key and scale of the production while maintaining the desired vocal style.

The amount of correction depends on the artistic goal. In this mix, Auto-Tune was part of the vocal sound as well as a corrective tool.

### 5. Comp FET-76 — Dynamic Compression

Comp FET-76 was used as the main character compressor.

A FET-style compressor is useful for vocals because it can respond quickly to peaks while adding energy and presence.

This stage was used to:

- Control loud vocal peaks
- Reduce excessive dynamic variation
- Keep quieter words more audible
- Add density and forwardness
- Help the vocal remain consistent within the instrumental

The compression was intended to make the performance more stable without removing all natural movement.

### 6. Fruity Limiter — Additional Level Control

Fruity Limiter was used after the main compressor for further peak and level management.

This stage helped:

- Catch peaks that passed through the FET compressor
- Prevent sudden level spikes
- Maintain a more controlled output level
- Improve consistency before saturation and effects

This provided a second stage of dynamic control rather than relying on a single compressor to perform every task.

### 7. Fruity WaveShaper — Saturation and Harmonic Shaping

Fruity WaveShaper was used to introduce controlled harmonic saturation.

This processing can help a vocal feel:

- Fuller
- More present
- More energetic
- Better integrated with the instrumental

The saturation was kept controlled to avoid audible distortion or excessive harshness.

### 8. ValhallaVintageVerb — Reverb

ValhallaVintageVerb was used to create depth and space around the vocal.

The reverb helped place the vocal within the production rather than leaving it completely dry and disconnected from the instrumental.

The main goals were to:

- Add atmosphere
- Create a sense of depth
- Blend the vocal into the musical environment
- Preserve enough clarity for the words to remain understandable

The reverb level was balanced so that the vocal still remained forward in the mix.

### 9. Fruity Parametric EQ 2 — Post-Effect Shaping

A third EQ instance was placed after the reverb stage.

This EQ was used to shape the overall processed vocal signal after compression, saturation, and spatial effects.

Possible uses at this stage include:

- Controlling low-frequency buildup introduced by effects
- Reducing excess brightness or harshness
- Refining the final vocal tone
- Preventing the processed signal from masking other parts of the mix

This stage provided final tonal control over the combined result.

### 10. Fruity Delay 2 — Delay and Spatial Enhancement

Fruity Delay 2 was used as the final spatial effect.

The delay added rhythmic depth and helped the vocal interact with the tempo and space of the production.

Depending on the section, the delay can contribute:

- Width
- Repetition
- Rhythmic movement
- Depth without relying entirely on reverb
- Additional emphasis on selected phrases

The delay was kept balanced so that it supported the vocal without reducing intelligibility.

## Processing Strategy

The chain was designed in stages:

1. Clean and shape the raw tone
2. Control harsh resonances
3. Correct pitch
4. Manage dynamics
5. Add harmonic character
6. Create space and depth
7. Refine the final processed tone

This order allowed each processor to address a specific problem rather than relying on one plugin to perform multiple unrelated tasks.

## Before-and-After Evaluation

The processed version was evaluated against the raw version for:

- Tonal balance
- Pitch consistency
- Vocal clarity
- Dynamic stability
- Harshness and resonance
- Presence in the mix
- Depth and spatial placement
- Saturation and harmonic character
- Overall intelligibility

## Result

The final vocal mix is more controlled, polished, and integrated with the instrumental.

Compared with the before version, the processed vocal has:

- More consistent pitch
- More stable dynamics
- Reduced harsh resonances
- Greater presence
- More harmonic density
- Improved depth and space
- Better placement within the production
